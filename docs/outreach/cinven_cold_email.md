Subject: Following up from Steve Taylor: a UK buyout screener built around Cinven's entries

Hi Samy,

Steve Taylor introduced us in October. I'm a junior at Duke studying economics and computer science, spending this term at King's College London, and I have built a sourcing screener around Cinven's own entry pattern that I'd value your view on.

It reads every active UK company filing full or group accounts on Companies House, traces the owner through the PSC register, sizes each one from its filed accounts against the two bands Cinven's entries since 2015 occupy (the Strategic Fund 2 range and the flagship fund range, with club mega-deals, minority stakes and US-led deals measured and left out), and scores the business on a rubric adapted from Cinven's published investment approach. Eight companies from its ranked list of 91:

1. Psychiatry UK (Camelford): digital psychiatry provider to the NHS; turnover of £160M for the 18 months to August 2025; Queen's Park Equity has been on the register since September 2024.
2. Wood Mackenzie (Edinburgh): energy data and analytics; turnover of £299M in 2025; Veritas Capital bought it from Verisk in February 2023.
3. Uniserve (Upminster): freight forwarding and trade management; turnover of £244M, up 21%, and operating profit of £26M in 2024; one owner, aged about 63.
4. Wernick Group (Wickford): modular building and site accommodation hire; operating profit of £40M in 2025; owned by three members of the Wernick family.
5. Swanton Care (Coventry): specialist care; turnover of £136M, up 39%, in 2025; Apposite Capital has been on the register for nine years.
6. Wilson Power Solutions (Leeds): power and distribution transformers; turnover of £86M and EBITDA of ~£19M in the period to March 2025; family-owned.
7. Mattioli Woods (Leicester): wealth management and pensions; turnover of £134M in the year to May 2025; Pollen Street Capital took it private in 2024.
8. Evolution Money (Manchester): second-charge mortgage lender; turnover of £56M in the year to March 2026; founder-owned.

One honest gap: only two of the 91 are in Financial Services, because most UK financial groups at this size file scanned accounts that the screener has only begun to read.
